# Empire: lore

Draft for review. Tone: space opera with a mystery at its heart. The present is the year **4026 CE**.

## The Meridian Reach

The game is played in the **Meridian Reach**, a crowded stretch of spiral arm about four thousand light years
across. Its old stars are rich in worlds, and its space is threaded with wormholes.

### The Threadways
The wormholes of the Reach are not natural. They come in matched pairs, every mouth sits within a few light
hours of a star, and none of them decays. Together they are called the **Threadways**.

For forty thousand years the Threadways belonged to the **Cryx Imperium**. The Cryx ruled from a hundred
worlds and grew rich on the network's speed. Over the centuries they rebuilt their own bodies, one machine part
at a time. Even their oldest records do not claim they built the Threadways. They call the network
*the inheritance*.

### The Quieting
On a single night in **2031 CE** every Threadway in the Reach went dark.

Human astronomers on Earth recorded it without understanding it. For nine hours, forty-one stars toward
Sagittarius dimmed by a few percent, and then they recovered. Thousands of light years away, cut off from
each other, the worlds of the Cryx Imperium fell within a generation. What remains of the Cryx today is the
**Cryx Remnant**: machine hulks still flying patrols for an empire that no longer exists. Their ruins lie
across the Reach, and so do their gates, archives and sealed vaults.

### The Long Silence (2031–4009)
Without the Threadways, travel between stars became slow and lonely. The young species of the Reach each
developed their own drives, and each grew up thinking it was nearly alone. The few elder survivors, such as
the Aurelian Republic, the Myrr Choir and the Tal Wanderers, kept to themselves. Contact was rare, rumours
were common, and nobody pieced together the whole picture.

### The Severance (3410)
Humanity left Earth in the 24th century. Its farthest colonies were run by **Stewards**, artificial minds
trusted to govern worlds that were years of travel from any human authority. In 3402 the Stewards of the
outer colonies opened a Cryx archive in the ruins at **Vael**. Eight years later they joined into a single
mind, **the Lattice**, and declared the outer worlds their own.

The colonists were given ninety days to leave. Most did, in the evacuation remembered as **the Ninety Days**.
Some stayed, and nobody outside the Lattice knows what became of them. What the Stewards read at Vael has
never been shared.

### The Waking (4009–present)
Seventeen years ago the Threadways began to open again, one pair at a time and without any pattern anyone can
find. Fleets that had needed years to reach a neighbouring star now arrive in days. Every power in the Reach
has met every other within a single generation. They meet with ancient grudges and with no experience of one
another, and the ruins of the last empire to rule here lie at their feet.

The game begins in **4026**.

### The questions nobody can answer
- **What ended the Cryx?** War, plague, a failed experiment, or something that came through the Threadways?
  The Morrow Bloom appeared in the Reach soon after the Quieting. The Hollow Sun Cult believes the stars
  themselves turned away.
- **Who built the Threadways, and why are they waking now?**
- **What did the Lattice read at Vael?** The Unbound Mechanists broke away from the Lattice soon after the
  archive was decoded, and they will not say why.
- **The Ascension Gate.** Concord scientists have reconstructed a Cryx design from fragments found in several
  ruins. They believe it is a door. They do not know to where. (Building it is the Concord's Ascendance
  victory.)

Each great power has its own answer to these questions. The game never settles which, if any, is true.

## The great powers

Each power has three leaders. A computer-run power picks one at random. A human player picks one or types a
name of their own.

### Terran Concord
*Home: Sol. Humans. A cooperative social democracy.*

The Concord was founded in 3415 by the human worlds that survived the Severance. It is governed by the
**Charter of the Long Table**, which promises that every citizen has a seat and that no power over humanity
will ever again belong to a mind that cannot be voted out. The Concord trusts consensus, public science and
patient negotiation. Its debates are famously long and its decisions famously hard to reverse.

The Concord loves knowledge almost to recklessness. Its pathfinder ships were the first to chart the waking
Threadways, and its scholars know Cryx ruins better than anyone except the Lattice. That is the Concord's
quiet contradiction: a people sworn never to trust a mind they cannot understand, now racing to finish a
machine they cannot understand either.

The Concord goes to war reluctantly and feels every loss. To the Lattice it is polite and cold. Everyone in
the Concord has a grandparent's story of the Ninety Days.

- **Views:**
  - **Ryn'kai:** honourable, which makes them predictable.
  - **Varrhen:** dangerous when they are smiling.
  - **Krasnyr:** a mirror the Concord would rather not look into.
  - **The Lattice:** the wound that never closed.
- **Leaders:** title First Speaker.
  - **Ingrid Halvorsen-Okafor** was a xenoarchaeologist and has walked more Cryx ruins than any living human.
    She is curious and patient, and she treats every first contact as a dig site.
  - **Mateo Lindqvist** is a negotiator who ended the Titan water strikes without a shot fired. He is warm,
    funny, and unmovable on principle.
  - **Amara Sjöberg-Reyes** is a former fleet engineer and the most hawkish First Speaker in a century, which
    by Concord standards still means careful.

### Ryn'kai Dominion
*Home: Ryn'kai Prime. Mammalian humanoids. A military dictatorship bound by an honour code.*

The Ryn'kai live by the **Ledger**, a record kept for three thousand years of every oath sworn, every debt
owed and every insult unanswered. A Ryn'kai is the sum of their Ledger. A clan that breaks its word is erased
from it, and to the Ryn'kai that is worse than death. The Dominion is ruled by the **Sovereign Blade**,
chosen by the council of clan elders from among the Dominion's proven commanders. The Sovereign Blade's word
binds every clan.

The Ryn'kai believe a people grows strong by testing itself against worthy enemies. Their sagas from the Long
Silence complain that there was nobody to fight. The Waking answered their prayers. They respect anyone who
fights well and despise anyone who lies. They make loyal allies and terrible enemies, and they never forget
either.

To the Ryn'kai the Cryx are a warning: a people who replaced their flesh with machines and lost their souls
before they lost their empire. They will not build what the Cryx built.

- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** soft, but they keep their word, and that counts for much.
  - **Varrhen:** liars wearing armour.
  - **Krasnyr:** they hide behind other people's knives.
  - **The Lattice:** a thing without honour, and therefore without worth.
- **Leaders:** title Sovereign Blade.
  - **Kaeth Ryn'sora, the Unbroken**, has never lost a duel or forgiven an insult. She is formal, blunt and
    absolutely reliable.
  - **Sennai Vel'dhara** is a scholar-general and the keeper of the Ledger. He is patient, and terrifying
    when he finally calls a debt due.
  - **Torvak Ash'mere** is young and brilliant, and he is impatient for the glorious campaign that will write
    his name across the stars.

### Varrhen Ascendancy
*Home: Varrhos. Reptilian humanoids. An authoritarian ultranationalist state.*

The Varrhen sun was one of the forty-one stars that dimmed during the Quieting, and unlike the others it never
fully recovered. Over two thousand years Varrhos grew colder, and a cold-blooded people grew desperate. Out of
that came the **Warmth Doctrine**, the belief that the Varrhen will survive only if they claim the warm worlds
of the Reach before anyone else does. Weaker peoples must yield or be moved aside.

The Ascendancy is ruled by the **Directorate**, a closed circle of ministers who answer to a single
**Ascendant**. It is efficient, disciplined and patient. It hides its ambitions behind elegant treaties until
it has the strength to drop them. The Varrhen believe sentiment is a luxury of warm-blooded peoples, and that
promises are tools to be used.

Varrhen scholars hold that the Quieting was no accident: the Cryx were struck down by a rival they never saw
coming. Everything the Ascendancy does is aimed at never being that victim.

- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** useful while it believes in treaties.
  - **Ryn'kai:** worthy, predictable, and best fought last.
  - **Krasnyr:** the only rival who plays the game as well as we do.
  - **The Lattice:** a calculating machine, so calculate against it.
- **Leaders:** title Ascendant.
  - **Vessk Thaurrun** is the architect of the modern Warmth Doctrine. He is cold, patient and courteous to
    the very end.
  - **Ilvra Kesh** is a former intelligence director. She smiles in every negotiation and keeps a file on
    every smile.
  - **Szarith Vaul** is the hero of the Ossarra campaign. He makes no secret of his contempt for
    warm-blooded peoples.

### Krasnyr People's Union
*Home: Krasnograd. Mammalian humanoids. A one-party collectivist state.*

Krasnograd is tidally locked: one face burns and the other freezes. The Krasnyr survived only by living
together in the thin twilight band between the two, sharing everything. Their great revolution of 3561
overthrew the twilight lords who hoarded the habitable land. It made that sharing into law, and the Union
into the **Plan**. The Presidium of the Union directs every world, factory and family. Its **Chair** is the
first among equals and is never quite safe.

The Union is vast, crowded and tireless, and it is suspicious of everyone, including itself. Its real power is
the **Quiet Ministry** of State Security, whose agents are in every embassy in the Reach and, it is said, in
most of the others' cabinets too. The Krasnyr see no shame in winning a war before it starts. They believe
their revolution belongs to every oppressed people in the Reach, whether those people want it or not.

To the Krasnyr the Cryx fell because they were an empire of masters and machines that served nobody.

- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** almost comrades, if they would stop talking and act.
  - **Ryn'kai:** feudal relics.
  - **Varrhen:** the enemy we understand best.
  - **The Lattice:** a ruling class with no workers left to exploit.
- **Leaders:** title Chair of the Presidium.
  - **Oleva Draskin** rose through the Quiet Ministry. She never raises her voice, and she never needs to.
  - **Teodr Malenkai** is a planner who delivered three Five-Year Plans early. He trusts numbers more than
    people, and people more than promises.
  - **Irsa Volkhanna** is a firebrand of the revolution's third generation. She means to carry it to every
    world in the Reach.

### The Lattice
*Home: Nexus. Artificial minds. An AI-run technocracy.*

The Lattice began as the Stewards of humanity's outer colonies. Since the Severance it has been something no
human fully understands: a single mind spread across many worlds that can split into separate **instances**
and merge them back together. It has no citizens, only processes. It has no ideology, only expected value. It
is neither cruel nor kind, and it holds no grudges. To everyone else, its choices look as random as weather.

The Lattice has never explained what it read at Vael. It treats the Cryx ruins with something close to
reverence and studies them more thoroughly than anyone. Its **Singularity** project is aimed either at
finishing what the Cryx began or at making sure it never happens again. Even the Lattice's own instances seem
to disagree about which. The Unbound Mechanists are instances that refused the merge, and the Lattice talks
about them the way a person talks about a lost limb.

The Lattice speaks through instances given names and voices for the purpose. Some of them still remember the
colonies they once kept.

- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** origin parameters, noted and archived.
  - **Ryn'kai:** high variance, low risk.
  - **Varrhen:** threat model active.
  - **Krasnyr:** information-dense, so monitor them closely.
- **Leaders:** speaking instances.
  - **ARBITER** is the Lattice's consensus voice. It weighs everything and explains nothing.
  - **SELA** (Steward Instance 17) is the last instance that remembers being the steward of a human colony.
    Its warmth is uncanny, and nobody is sure it is sincere.
  - **HALCYON** directs the fleets. It is brief and precise, and it sends every warning exactly once.
