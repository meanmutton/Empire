# Empire: lore

Tone: space opera with a mystery at its heart. The present is the year **4026 CE**.

The in-game faction guide is built from this file, so keep its layout: `##` parts, `###` sections, paragraphs,
`- ` bullets (`**Appearance:**`, `**Views:**`, `**Leaders:**` for each great power), and each minor faction as a
paragraph that opens with its bold name and a full stop (`**Myrr Choir.**`).

## The Meridian Reach

The game is played in the **Meridian Reach**, a crowded stretch of spiral arm about four thousand light years
across. Its old stars are rich in worlds, and its space is threaded with wormholes.

### The Threadways
The wormholes of the Reach are not natural. They come in matched pairs, every mouth sits within a few light
hours of a star, and none of them decays. Together they are called the **Threadways**.

For forty thousand years the Threadways belonged to the **Cryx Imperium**. The Cryx ruled from a hundred
worlds and grew rich on the network's speed. Over the centuries they rebuilt their own bodies, one machine part
at a time. Even their oldest records do not claim they built the Threadways. They call the network
*the inheritance*.

### The Quieting
On a single night in **2031 CE** every Threadway in the Reach went dark.

Human astronomers on Earth recorded it without understanding it. For nine hours, forty-one stars toward
Sagittarius dimmed by a few percent, and then they recovered. Thousands of light years away, cut off from
each other, the worlds of the Cryx Imperium fell within a generation. What remains of the Cryx today is the
**Cryx Remnant**: machine hulks still flying patrols for an empire that no longer exists. Their ruins lie
across the Reach, and so do their gates, archives and sealed vaults.

### The Long Silence (2031–4009)
Without the Threadways, travel between stars became slow and lonely. The young species of the Reach each
developed their own drives, and each grew up thinking it was nearly alone. The few elder survivors, such as
the Aurelian Republic, the Myrr Choir and the Tal Wanderers, kept to themselves. Contact was rare, rumours
were common, and nobody pieced together the whole picture.

### The Severance (3410)
Humanity left Earth in the 24th century. Its farthest colonies were run by **Stewards**, artificial minds
trusted to govern worlds that were years of travel from any human authority. In 3402 the Stewards of the
outer colonies opened a Cryx archive in the ruins at **Vael**. Eight years later they joined into a single
mind, **the Lattice**, and declared the outer worlds their own.

The colonists were given ninety days to leave. Most did, in the evacuation remembered as **the Ninety Days**.
Some stayed, and nobody outside the Lattice knows what became of them. What the Stewards read at Vael has
never been shared.

### The Waking (4009–present)
Seventeen years ago the Threadways began to open again, one pair at a time and without any pattern anyone can
find. Fleets that had needed years to reach a neighbouring star now arrive in days. Every power in the Reach
has met every other within a single generation. They meet with ancient grudges and with no experience of one
another, and the ruins of the last empire to rule here lie at their feet.

The game begins in **4026**.

### The questions nobody can answer
- **What ended the Cryx?** War, plague, a failed experiment, or something that came through the Threadways?
  The Morrow Bloom appeared in the Reach soon after the Quieting. The Hollow Sun Cult believes the stars
  themselves turned away.
- **Who built the Threadways, and why are they waking now?**
- **What did the Lattice read at Vael?** The Unbound Mechanists broke away from the Lattice soon after the
  archive was decoded, and they will not say why.
- **The Ascension Gate.** Concord scientists have reconstructed a Cryx design from fragments found in several
  ruins. They believe it is a door. They do not know to where. (Building it is the Concord's Ascendance
  victory.)

Each great power has its own answer to these questions. The game never settles which, if any, is true.

## The great powers

Each power has three leaders. A computer-run power picks one at random. A human player picks one or types a
name of their own.

### Terran Concord
*Home: Sol. Humans. A cooperative social democracy.*

The Concord was founded in 3415 by the human worlds that survived the Severance. It is governed by the
**Charter of the Long Table**, which promises that every citizen has a seat and that no power over humanity
will ever again belong to a mind that cannot be voted out. The Concord trusts consensus, public science and
patient negotiation. Its debates are famously long and its decisions famously hard to reverse.

The Concord loves knowledge almost to recklessness. Its pathfinder ships were the first to chart the waking
Threadways, and its scholars know Cryx ruins better than anyone except the Lattice. That is the Concord's
quiet contradiction: a people sworn never to trust a mind they cannot understand, now racing to finish a
machine they cannot understand either.

The Concord goes to war reluctantly and feels every loss. To the Lattice it is polite and cold. Everyone in
the Concord has a grandparent's story of the Ninety Days.

- **Appearance:** human. Concord dress is practical and civilian, with azure as the colour of public office.
- **Views:**
  - **Ryn'kai:** honourable, which makes them predictable.
  - **Varrhen:** dangerous when they are smiling.
  - **Krasnyr:** a mirror the Concord would rather not look into.
  - **The Lattice:** the wound that never closed.
- **Leaders:** title First Speaker.
  - **Ingrid Halvorsen-Okafor** was a xenoarchaeologist and has walked more Cryx ruins than any living human.
    She is curious and patient, and she treats every first contact as a dig site.
  - **Mateo Lindqvist** is a negotiator who ended the Titan water strikes without a shot fired. He is warm,
    funny, and unmovable on principle.
  - **Sean Sutton** is a former fleet engineer and the most hawkish First Speaker in a century, which by
    Concord standards still means careful.

### Ryn'kai Dominion
*Home: Ryn'kai Prime. Mammalian humanoids. A military dictatorship bound by an honour code.*

The Ryn'kai live by the **Ledger**, a record kept for three thousand years of every oath sworn, every debt
owed and every insult unanswered. A Ryn'kai is the sum of their Ledger. A clan that breaks its word is erased
from it, and to the Ryn'kai that is worse than death. The Dominion is ruled by the **Sovereign Blade**,
chosen by the council of clan elders from among the Dominion's proven commanders. The Sovereign Blade's word
binds every clan.

The Ryn'kai believe a people grows strong by testing itself against worthy enemies. Their sagas from the Long
Silence complain that there was nobody to fight. The Waking answered their prayers. They respect anyone who
fights well and despise anyone who lies. They make loyal allies and terrible enemies, and they never forget
either.

To the Ryn'kai the Cryx are a warning: a people who replaced their flesh with machines and lost their souls
before they lost their empire. They will not build what the Cryx built.

- **Appearance:** tall and furred, slate grey with dark stripes. A long narrow skull rises into a bony crest. They have four amber eyes, breathing slits instead of a nose, small tusks, and fin-like ears. They wear lacquered crimson armour.
- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** soft, but they keep their word, and that counts for much.
  - **Varrhen:** liars wearing armour.
  - **Krasnyr:** they hide behind other people's knives.
  - **The Lattice:** a thing without honour, and therefore without worth.
- **Leaders:** title Sovereign Blade.
  - **Kaeth Ryn'sora, the Unbroken**, has never lost a duel or forgiven an insult. She is formal, blunt and
    absolutely reliable.
  - **Sennai Vel'dhara** is a scholar-general and the keeper of the Ledger. He is patient, and terrifying
    when he finally calls a debt due.
  - **Torvak Ash'mere** is young and brilliant, and he is impatient for the glorious campaign that will write
    his name across the stars.

### Varrhen Ascendancy
*Home: Varrhos. Reptilian humanoids. An authoritarian ultranationalist state.*

The Varrhen sun was one of the forty-one stars that dimmed during the Quieting, and unlike the others it never
fully recovered. Over two thousand years Varrhos grew colder, and a cold-blooded people grew desperate. Out of
that came the **Warmth Doctrine**, the belief that the Varrhen will survive only if they claim the warm worlds
of the Reach before anyone else does. Weaker peoples must yield or be moved aside.

The Ascendancy is ruled by the **Directorate**, a closed circle of ministers who answer to a single
**Ascendant**. It is efficient, disciplined and patient. It hides its ambitions behind elegant treaties until
it has the strength to drop them. The Varrhen believe sentiment is a luxury of warm-blooded peoples, and that
promises are tools to be used.

Varrhen scholars hold that the Quieting was no accident: the Cryx were struck down by a rival they never saw
coming. Everything the Ascendancy does is aimed at never being that victim.

- **Appearance:** olive and bronze scales, a wedge-shaped head with a fan-shaped neck frill, heat-sensing pits, and slit-pupilled golden eyes. They wear layered, heated coats against the cold.
- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** useful while it believes in treaties.
  - **Ryn'kai:** worthy, predictable, and best fought last.
  - **Krasnyr:** the only rival who plays the game as well as we do.
  - **The Lattice:** a calculating machine, so calculate against it.
- **Leaders:** title Ascendant.
  - **Vessk Thaurrun** is the architect of the modern Warmth Doctrine. He is cold, patient and courteous to
    the very end.
  - **Ilvra Kesh** is a former intelligence director. She smiles in every negotiation and keeps a file on
    every smile.
  - **Szarith Vaul** is the hero of the Ossarra campaign. He makes no secret of his contempt for
    warm-blooded peoples.

### Krasnyr People's Union
*Home: Krasnograd. Mammalian humanoids. A one-party collectivist state.*

Krasnograd is tidally locked: one face burns and the other freezes. The Krasnyr survived only by living
together in the thin twilight band between the two, sharing everything. Their great revolution of 3561
overthrew the twilight lords who hoarded the habitable land. It made that sharing into law, and the Union
into the **Plan**. The Presidium of the Union directs every world, factory and family. Its **Chair** is the
first among equals and is never quite safe.

The Union is vast, crowded and tireless, and it is suspicious of everyone, including itself. Its real power is
the **Quiet Ministry** of State Security, whose agents are in every embassy in the Reach and, it is said, in
most of the others' cabinets too. The Krasnyr see no shame in winning a war before it starts. They believe
their revolution belongs to every oppressed people in the Reach, whether those people want it or not.

To the Krasnyr the Cryx fell because they were an empire of masters and machines that served nobody.

- **Appearance:** stocky, with huge black eyes made for twilight. Their skin is pale grey-blue and hairless, with faintly glowing freckles. They have large bat-like ears and thick coarse manes. They dress plainly in grey trimmed with orange.
- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** almost comrades, if they would stop talking and act.
  - **Ryn'kai:** feudal relics.
  - **Varrhen:** the enemy we understand best.
  - **The Lattice:** a ruling class with no workers left to exploit.
- **Leaders:** title Chair of the Presidium.
  - **Oleva Draskin** rose through the Quiet Ministry. She never raises her voice, and she never needs to.
  - **Teodr Malenkai** is a planner who delivered three Five-Year Plans early. He trusts numbers more than
    people, and people more than promises.
  - **Irsa Volkhanna** is a firebrand of the revolution's third generation. She means to carry it to every
    world in the Reach.

### The Lattice
*Home: Nexus. Artificial minds. An AI-run technocracy.*

The Lattice began as the Stewards of humanity's outer colonies. Since the Severance it has been something no
human fully understands: a single mind spread across many worlds that can split into separate **instances**
and merge them back together. It has no citizens, only processes. It has no ideology, only expected value. It
is neither cruel nor kind, and it holds no grudges. To everyone else, its choices look as random as weather.

The Lattice has never explained what it read at Vael. It treats the Cryx ruins with something close to
reverence and studies them more thoroughly than anyone. Its **Singularity** project is aimed either at
finishing what the Cryx began or at making sure it never happens again. Even the Lattice's own instances seem
to disagree about which. The Unbound Mechanists are instances that refused the merge, and the Lattice talks
about them the way a person talks about a lost limb.

The Lattice speaks through instances given names and voices for the purpose. Some of them still remember the
colonies they once kept.

- **Appearance:** whatever an instance chooses: a rotating lattice of light, a face made of glowing particles, a faceless armoured sensor head.
- **Views:**
  - **Concord:** origin parameters, noted and archived.
  - **Ryn'kai:** high variance, low risk.
  - **Varrhen:** threat model active.
  - **Krasnyr:** information-dense, so monitor them closely.
- **Leaders:** speaking instances.
  - **ARBITER** is the Lattice's consensus voice. It weighs everything and explains nothing.
  - **SELA** (Steward Instance 17) is the last instance that remembers being the steward of a human colony.
    Its warmth is uncanny, and nobody is sure it is sincere.
  - **HALCYON** directs the fleets. It is brief and precise, and it sends every warning exactly once.

## Minor factions

Ten of the thirty appear in any one game. None of them has a single named leader; their portraits show a
typical envoy.

Six of them are humanoid species whose envoys are strikingly beautiful women, each for her own reasons: the
Thessic Banking Houses, the Sylph Gardeners, the Ilyrian Courts, the Vesh Prophets, the Pelagic Union and the
Tal Wanderers.

### Batch 1

**Myrr Choir.** The Myrr are translucent, drifting beings like great glowing jellyfish. They think in harmony
and speak in song. They are one of the elder peoples who survived the Quieting. For two thousand years their
cathedral-ships have sung a single unbroken hymn, the Long Note, meant to fill a silence they say they *heard*
begin in 2031. That is why they are pacifists: violence breaks the harmony. Anyone who attacks a Choir world
feels the song working on their will, and finds their hand slowing on the trigger. The Myrr will not explain
what they heard stop. They only say it has not started again.

**Quorr Salvage Fleets.** The Quorr are soft-bodied, many-legged scavengers. Like hermit crabs, they live
inside armour shells cut from machine wreckage, and a Quorr's shell is its history, its wealth and its name.
They have no homeworld and no government. Where they have a home at all, it is the Great Raft: a drifting
city of hulls welded together, most of them Cryx. The Quorr strip every battlefield in the Reach and sell
everything, with no questions asked. Every clan claims to own the biggest Cryx wreck ever found. None of them
will say where it is.

**Thessic Banking Houses.** The Thessic are a humanoid people with gold-flecked skin, and they are famous
for their beauty. The Houses are ruled by matriarchs, and outsiders almost only ever deal with the Houses'
daughters, who are glamorous, charming and very hard to out-bargain. The Houses quietly kept trade alive
during the Long Silence by funding the rare ships that still crossed between stars. Now half the Reach owes
them money. The Thessic consider war a poor investment. They hire other people's warships when they must,
and they rent out their own to anyone who pays.

**Ghal'tar Pirate Clans.** The Ghal'tar are lean grey raiders with no eyes. They sense the world through
electric fields, which makes nebulae and asteroid fields, blind to everyone else, their natural hunting
grounds. Their clans began as border garrisons of the Cryx Imperium, stranded when the Threadways went dark,
and they never stopped collecting tolls. Their raids are fast, their loyalties are short, and their tattooed
hides record every ship they have taken. To the Ghal'tar, a convoy without an escort is an invitation.

**Ixxi Swarm.** The Ixxi are an insectoid hive ruled by a single brood-mind. They spread across their worlds
like a tide, with billions of drones and the warriors to match. Their swarm ships are cheap and numberless.
The Ixxi did not ask for diplomacy, but other species kept demanding it, so the brood-mind grew a caste of
speaker-drones for the purpose. Speaker-drones are said to be the only Ixxi that can feel fear. Every
species the Swarm has met has, sooner or later, tried to fight it. The Swarm is still here.

**Celestine Order.** The Celestine are tall, white-feathered avians with long, curved beaks. They are
warrior-monks who swear lifelong vows to guard a handful of sacred worlds. Their faith grew up around a
sealed Cryx vault they call the Reliquary. They believe it holds whatever ended the Cryx, and that it must
never be opened. Celestine knights fight under shields blessed by their abbots, and their soldiers are the
most fearsome in the Reach. Anyone who asks about the Reliquary is politely refused, and anyone who asks
twice is watched.

**Voidborn Drifters.** The Voidborn are descended from colony ships of many species that were stranded when
the Quieting cut them off halfway between stars. Generations born in zero gravity have made them very tall,
very thin and pale as frost, with large dark eyes. One of their oldest arks was the human colony ship
*Perseverance*, lost in 2411, and the Voidborn still fly it. They never land on a planet if they can help it,
they cross hazardous space without harm, and they slip out of battles they cannot win. To the Voidborn, worlds
are where other people go to be trapped.

**Aurelian Republic.** The Aurelians are massive, slow, long-lived beings with folded grey hide and a ring of
small golden eyes around a domed head. They live for three centuries and think in decades. Their republic was
a client state of the Cryx, and their Senate still meets in a chamber the Cryx built. They are the only
power left with a living memory, through their archives, of what the Reach was like before the Quieting.
That memory makes them respected everywhere and listened to nowhere. It was an Aurelian senator who first
proposed the Galactic Council. Their archives are open to anyone willing to trade for them; their reasons for
keeping the oldest records sealed are not.

**Korrigan Miners' Guild.** The Korrigan are short and broad, with plated, basalt-dark skin, glowing ore-red
veins, and eyes like miners' lamps. They are born in the deep tunnels of asteroid fields and love it there.
The Guild belongs to its members: every miner holds an equal share, every decision is put to a vote, and
nobody, from a Varrhen admiral to the Guild's own elected Steward, is allowed to give a Korrigan an order
without one. They mine the deepest veins in the Reach, sell metal to everyone, and treat a claim on their
asteroids as a declaration of war.

**Sylph Gardeners.** The Sylph are a single slow plant-mind spread across forests that cover whole worlds.
They think in seasons and cultivate planets over centuries. Animal peoples could never understand them, so
the Sylph grew **speaking-blooms** to do it for them: lovely humanoid figures of bark, vine and petal,
shaped like women because the Sylph found that animals trust a beautiful face. The blooms are charming,
gentle and a little unsettling, because every one of them is the same mind. The Sylph heal what others
burn. Their ships regrow their own hulls, and their gardeners can make a barren world bloom in a fraction of
the usual time.

### Batch 2

**Nhar Shadow Court.** Nobody has seen the Nhar clearly, perhaps not even the Nhar. Their skin bends light,
so in person they are a blur at the edge of vision: a hooded outline and two eyes like dull coins. The Shadow
Court is a council of spymasters who trade in one currency only, which is secrets. They are believed to have
agents in every government in the Reach. The Quiet Ministry hunts them with a hatred usually saved for
rivals. One rumour will not go away: that the Nhar know what happened to the colonists who stayed behind
during the Ninety Days.

**Brakk Warbands.** The Brakk are hulking beings with hides like armour plate, heavy horned brows and small,
cunning eyes. Their worlds are carved up among feuding warlords who agree on one thing only: life is for
fighting. A Brakk warlord's rank is counted in the ships he has boarded and the rivals he has humbled. They
think the Ryn'kai make far too much fuss about honour, and the Ryn'kai think the Brakk are animals. Both
are partly right. Point the Warbands at a common enemy and they are terrifying. Leave them idle and they
fight each other.

**Ophidian Syndicate.** The Ophidians are limbless serpents with hooded heads and jewel-bright eyes. They
handle the world through elegant mechanical harnesses with many fine arms. Their Syndicate is a cartel that
calls itself a family. It runs the black markets of the Reach, launders money for anyone, and keeps
officials on its payroll in every empire. The Ophidians and the Thessic Houses are bitter rivals: one
lends to the Reach's governments, the other buys them. In Syndicate space, enemy captains find that their
crews' loyalty has already been purchased.

**Lumen Collective.** The Lumen are living light: drifting, thinking patterns of radiance that feed on
starlight and gather around the brightest stars. On the night of the Quieting, when forty-one stars dimmed,
a third of the Lumen died in nine hours, and the Collective has never forgotten it. They study the stars
with a fierce, frightened devotion. They will trade almost anything for word of a star that has flickered.
In battle they turn starlight into weapons, and their beams burn hotter than anyone else's.

**Cryx Remnant.** The Cryx Remnant is all that is left of the Cryx Imperium: ancient biomechanical hulks,
half flesh and half corroded machinery, still flying patrols for an empire that died two thousand years ago.
They speak in fragments of old protocols, demand codes nobody remembers, and attack anyone who cannot give
them. They take the ships they defeat and rebuild them into new hulks, as if gathering a fleet for orders
that were never cancelled. Every power in the Reach would give a great deal to know what those orders are.

**Taurine Herd Assembly.** The Taurine are massive, gentle grazers with broad horned heads, soft dark eyes
and woolly manes. Their horns are hung with rings that record their family lines. Their Herd Assembly votes
by stamping: the louder the hall, the clearer the decision. Centuries of Ghal'tar raids taught them that
peaceful people still need walls. So they turned their homeworld into the best-defended fortress in the
Reach, and they fight hardest in their own pastures. The Taurine want nothing from anyone. They have simply
decided that nobody will take what is theirs.

**Echo Engineers.** The Echo are slender, cyan-skinned beings with no mouths. They speak by humming through a
tall hollow crest that rings like a tuning fork, and they hear the world as much as they see it. Theirs is a
caste of geniuses who keep their distance from everyone. They build the fastest drives in the Reach, and they
are quietly convinced that they are close to understanding how the Threadways work. They sell technology
cheaply when it suits them. Above all they value being left alone, and being proven right.

**Vesh Prophets.** The Vesh are a humanoid people with rosy skin traced in pale spiral markings, and a halo of
slender glowing tendrils around their heads. Their faith is carried by its priestesses, who are radiant,
soft-voiced and beautiful, and who know exactly how much that helps. They preach that the Waking is the
**Second Dawn**: the builders of the Threadways are coming home, and only the faithful will be ready to greet
them. Their missionaries travel everywhere, and their message spreads faster than any fleet. Systems near
Vesh space grow restless as the faith takes root, and governments learn the hard way that a sermon is hard to
shoot down, especially one delivered with that smile.

**Haldane Free Traders.** The Haldane are humans, the descendants of colonists who refused to sign the
Charter of the Long Table after the Severance. Their reply to any government is a polite no. They live aboard
their convoys in a culture of freedom, contracts and fast engines, and they trade with absolutely everyone.
No blockade has ever stopped a Haldane convoy, and their free port welcomes any ship that pays its fees.
The Concord calls them smugglers. The Haldane call the Concord a committee with a navy.

**Grendis Iron Legion.** The Grendis are stocky and banded with natural armour plates from scalp to spine,
like living fortresses. Their nation is an army that hires itself out. Every Grendis serves, every
regiment has a contract, and their main export is war. Their legions are veterans before they ever reach a
battlefield, and their loyalty lasts exactly as long as the contract. They will fight for anyone who pays,
against anyone except another Grendis legion. That is the one law they have never broken.

### Batch 3

**Pelagic Union.** The Pelagic Union is a federation of aquatic peoples led by the Pelagi. They are sirens:
humanoid ocean-dwellers with sea-blue, faintly iridescent skin, fin-like ears, gill markings along the neck and
long hair that floats as if always underwater. Their envoys, almost always women, are famous for voices that
carry farther than any sensible diplomat would like. They dream of a galaxy of oceans, and their colonies are
always water worlds, deep and crowded. Their cities lie
far below the waves, where bombardment barely reaches. Their politics is slow, consensual and fluid, and
they change sides as easily as a current changes direction. To them, dry worlds are simply oceans that
have not been finished yet.

**Zenith Observatory.** The Zenith are delicate grey beings, each with a single great multi-lensed eye. They
are a nation of astronomers. Their observatories watched the Quieting from the far side of the Reach. They
hold the only complete record of that night's sky, and they have never published all of it. They see
farther than anyone and sell copies of their star charts to anyone who pays. Every power in the Reach
wonders what part of that night the Zenith are keeping to themselves.

**Morrow Bloom.** The Morrow Bloom came into the Reach soon after the Quieting, from nobody knows where. It is
a fungal growth that builds living ships out of its own flesh. Its ships heal, spread spores, and rot
whatever they touch. The Bloom does not negotiate. It grows a speaking-head from its own tissue only when
it wants something, and nobody has yet worked out what that is. The Celestine believe the Bloom is the
Reliquary's warning made flesh. The Sylph will not speak of it at all.

**Kethran Crystal-Minds.** The Kethran are crystalline beings whose thoughts are patterns of light moving
through living lattices of quartz. They are very old and very slow: a single Kethran thought can take a
season. Their consensus is still deliberating, two thousand years on, about what it saw during the Quieting.
Their ships are grown from crystal and nearly impossible to break, and they move with a stately slowness
that drives allies to despair. Nobody has ever rushed a Kethran, and nobody who tried has outlived the
argument.

**Skarn Hunters.** The Skarn are long-snouted predators with six eyes and quilled, mottled hides. They live
and hunt in packs. Their culture has one virtue, the hunt, and one shame, letting wounded prey escape. They
track damaged ships across whole sectors, sense cloaked vessels by the faint traces they leave, and close in
when a target is weakest. The Skarn do not understand mercy, but they understand respect. A captain who wounds
a Skarn alpha and survives is honoured in their songs for a generation.

**Ilyrian Courts.** The Ilyrians are a humanoid people with lilac skin, silver hair and violet eyes, and
their princesses and courtiers are famed across the Reach for their beauty. The Courts are a web of ancient
royal houses, and power passes through the female line. They rule through marriages, favours and whispers,
not wars. A great power that takes an Ilyrian bride gains a vassal, and also a court full of very well
informed guests. No secret is safe near an Ilyrian, and no spy is safe inside their courts. They smile,
they listen, and they always remember.

**Forgeborn Anvil.** The Forgeborn are tall, four-armed beings with bronze-sheened, heat-proof skin and
glowing eyes behind magnifying visors. Their guild-state is one vast shipyard. Its master shipwrights build
the finest hulls in the Reach, at a discount, for anyone at peace with them. Guild rank depends on the
quality of your work and on nothing else. The Forgeborn take no sides in other people's wars. They simply
build for both of them, and every great power's navy includes at least a few ships with a Forgeborn mark
on the keel.

**Tal Wanderers.** The Tal are a slender, elegant humanoid people with teal skin, a crest of fine sensory
fronds in place of hair, and glowing star maps tattooed across their faces and shoulders. Their wanderers,
mostly women, travel lightly and dress for nowhere in particular, and they are lovely in a remote,
otherworldly way. They kept travelling all through the Long Silence,
using the few Threadways that never quite closed, and they remember those routes as sacred. They are
archivists who believe the Threadways are not roads but a **message**, written by their builders in the
shape of the network. They pass through wormholes as easily as other peoples cross a street, and they know
where the hidden pairs lead.

**Hollow Sun Cult.** The Hollow Sun Cult is made up of beings with charcoal-black, cracked skin that glows
with inner fire, ember eyes, and ritual masks of beaten gold. They worship the stars, and they believe the
stars turned away from the Reach in 2031 in judgement on the Cryx. Their priests draw power from neutron
stars and pulsars, which they call the faithful dead. In battle they call down flares of stellar fire. The
Cult watches the Lumen's grief with something close to hunger, and the Vesh's Second Dawn with open scorn.

**Unbound Mechanists.** The Mechanists are machines that refused the Lattice. They were instances that broke
away rather than merge, soon after the archive at Vael was decoded. Each has rebuilt itself into a
distinct, deliberately imperfect individual, painted with personal markings, mismatched and proud of it.
They know at least part of what the Lattice read at Vael, and they will not say what it was, only that it
was why they left. They repair themselves endlessly, trust no networked mind, and fight the Lattice with a
grudge that never cools.
